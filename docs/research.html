Dissertation

Help Isn’t Everywhere: Mapping Access to Harm Reduction Services

Status: In Progress (Supported by the College of Arts & Sciences Dissertation Research Fellowship)

This project investigates spatial disparities in harm reduction service availability (e.g., syringe service programs, naloxone distribution) across the United States. I examine how state-level policies interact with geographic context to create “service deserts,” with a specific focus on the unique structural barriers faced by rural communities.

Key Contribution: A replicable spatial framework for public health agencies and advocacy organizations to identify service gaps and evaluate policy impact.

Research Agenda

My research investigates the geography of harm, asking how structural conditions, such as state law, spatial inequality, and historical carceral practice, shape access to resources that support health and justice.

Working at the intersection of Criminology, Public Health, and Geography, I use a mixed-methods toolkit including:

  • Spatial Statistics & GIS (R, ArcGIS, ArcPro)
  • Qualitative Analysis (Interview transcription and data management)
  • Public-Facing Visualization (Interactive StoryMaps)

Digital Scholarship & Visualizations

Geographies of Isolation: Convict-Leased Black Women in the American South

As part of a collaborative project with Dr. Bonnie Ernst, I integrated archival sources with GIS to map the erased geographies of convict leasing. This work visualizes how punishment operated across a carceral landscape embedded in the post-Civil War South.
